# Annotated core sources

Compiled 2026-11-14. Where publication dates are not established, use n.d. in a formal citation. Public links are retained rather than redistributing full publications.

## S1 — Clear steps
**Citation:** World Wide Web Consortium, Web Accessibility Initiative. (2021; web interface 2022). *Use Clear Step-by-step Instructions*. Supplemental Guidance to WCAG 2.

https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o4p07-step-instructions/

**Type/review:** Primary accessibility guidance; page reviewed.
**Finding:** Instructions should be concise, situated near the activity, and broken into steps without omissions.
**Signal implication:** One actionable step with essential constraints retained; original wording available.
**Limit:** Supplemental cognitive guidance is not itself a WCAG conformance requirement or a controlled trial of Signal. It does not establish an ideal summary length.

## S2 — Control interruptions
**Citation:** World Wide Web Consortium, Web Accessibility Initiative. (n.d.). *Limit Interruptions*. Supplemental Guidance to WCAG 2.

https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o5p01-minimal-interruptions/

**Type/review:** Primary accessibility guidance; page reviewed.
**Finding:** Users should be able to control potentially distracting interruptions and changes.
**Signal implication:** No auto-advance, unexpected audio or unsolicited popups; student controls when to reveal detail.
**Limit:** Does not show that all students with ADHD need the same sensory environment. No efficacy percentage is justified.

## S3 — Progressive disclosure and presentation
**Citation:** World Wide Web Consortium, Web Accessibility Initiative. (n.d.). *Presentation: Distinguishing and Understanding*.

https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/tools-techniques/presentation/

**Type/review:** Primary accessibility guidance; consulted during design research.
**Finding:** Progressive disclosure presents what is needed for a task while allowing access to further detail.
**Signal implication:** Short orientation with expandable source material; do not hide essential requirements behind disclosure.
**Limit:** This informs our pattern; it does not establish that hiding content always improves comprehension.

## S4 — Focus guidance
**Citation:** World Wide Web Consortium, Web Accessibility Initiative. (n.d.). *Help Users Focus*. Supplemental Guidance to WCAG 2.

https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o5-user-focus/

**Type/review:** Primary design-guidance overview; consulted during design research.
**Signal implication:** Keep the route to the next action short and reduce unrelated competing content.
**Limit:** Our specific three-part screen and visual styling remain design hypotheses.

## S5 — GenAI task support with university students with ADHD
**Citation:** Zhu, Z., Yu, J., & Luo, Y. (2026). *Scaffolding Metacognition with GenAI: Exploring Design Opportunities to Support Task Management for University Students with ADHD*. arXiv:2602.09381. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2602.09381

https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381

**Type/review:** Primary exploratory co-design research; abstract and bibliographic record reviewed, not full-text appraisal.
**Finding:** Co-design sessions with 20 university students diagnosed with ADHD and interviews with five experts produced directions around cognitive scaffolding, reflective execution and emotional regulation.
**Signal implication:** Explore orientation, explicit next actions and supportive, nonjudgmental language with students.
**Limit:** Co-design findings are not proof that an AI summary improves attention, grades, or classroom participation. Our implementation differs from the study context.

## S6 — Preferences differ across people with ADHD
**Citation:** Zhu, H. 'Hazel', Chen, R., & Zhao, Y. (2025). *FocusView: Understanding and Customizing Informational Video Watching Experiences for Viewers with ADHD*. arXiv:2507.13309. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.13309

https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13309

**Type/review:** Primary interface study; abstract and bibliographic record reviewed, not full-text appraisal.
**Finding:** A study with 12 participants with ADHD explored video customization and reported differing perceptions of distractions and customization needs.
**Signal implication:** Offer a small number of student-controlled views rather than one mandatory presentation.
**Limit:** Video viewing is not classroom instruction resumption. Do not transfer reported benefits to Signal or claim a universal ADHD aesthetic.

## S7 — Disability prevalence context
**Citation:** National Center for Education Statistics. (n.d.). *Fast Facts: Students with disabilities*.

https://nces.ed.gov/fastfacts/display.asp?id=60

**Type/review:** Federal descriptive statistics; previously consulted in project research. Re-fetch on 2026-11-14 timed out.
**Finding recorded in our notes:** 21% of undergraduates reported a disability in 2019–20 under the survey definition.
**Use:** Broad context, with year and denominator stated.
**Limit:** Not ADHD prevalence, current-year prevalence, or a count of students helped by Signal. Recheck source before putting this statistic on the final deck.

## S8 — Postsecondary disability barriers and main-condition categories
**Citation:** U.S. Government Accountability Office. (2024). *Higher Education and Disability* (GAO-24-105614).

https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105614

Table source: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-105614.pdf (Appendix II, Table 4).

**Type/review:** Federal report and survey analysis; reviewed during earlier project research.
**Finding:** In the cited NPSAS 2020 analysis, attention deficit disorder is the main reported condition for 21% of undergraduates with disabilities; categories count the main condition, not every co-occurring condition. Report also discusses access and implementation barriers qualitatively.
**Use:** Explain why attention-related needs merit investigation; see ../disability-evidence.md for the full table.
**Limit:** Not 21% of all undergraduates. Qualitative findings do not give national barrier rates. Both federal recommendations were marked closed–implemented when previously checked; do not present those specific federal actions as still pending. Descriptive data does not establish product effectiveness.
