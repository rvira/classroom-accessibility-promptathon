## Ranked opportunities for our deadline
1. **Instruction access and resumption (best broad-reach feasible hypothesis).** A student with processing/executive-function barriers cannot recover the next action in an in-class task. Build an original-instructions pane and student-controlled next-step view, with each instruction tied to an exact source sentence and ambiguity explicit. Test with three activity prompts containing exceptions, changed deadlines and missing information. Measure requirement preservation and invented requirements; ask users whether it reduces effort. Existing Goblin Tools decomposes tasks and Genio/Otter support notes, so generic checklist/summary novelty is weak. Differentiation hypothesis: preserve teacher requirements and let the learner rejoin NOW. This is not treatment for ADHD or depression.
2. **Access to a specific chart (highest potential per-person access improvement).** A blind/screen-reader user cannot obtain essential values from an image-only slide. Restrict to bar charts for value-comparison questions; AI extracts labels/values into a table, instructor checks them, student explores with keyboard and screen reader. Existing Chart4Blind and image assistants mean conversion is not new; focus on verified classroom data and preserving the original question without giving the answer. Test exact extraction, unreadable values, keyboard and actual screen-reader behavior. Do not claim general diagram accessibility, arbitrary math support, or equivalence for visual-estimation assessments.
3. **Accommodations reaching actual course materials (system-level opportunity; harder today).** Teacher-facing draft conversion of one in-class handout to accessible HTML and clear instructions, keeping learning requirements intact. Existing Anthology Ally/Immersive Reader cover alternatives and reading supports. Product hypothesis: a reviewable change list for this lesson rather than an automatic accessibility/compliance score. Cannot solve documentation expense, faculty refusal or clinical needs with generated prose.
4. **Re-entry after fluctuating capacity (promising but transcript-dependent).** A student returns after fatigue, pain, overload or missed lecture segments; show the current concept, missed prerequisites, and source timestamps. Potential cross-condition benefit is a hypothesis. Requires accessible authorized source material; summaries can omit meaning and compete with established lecture apps. Scope to provided transcript, not live recording.
5. **Turn-taking access (severe for some, co-design essential).** A student who needs more time to compose a contribution requests a turn and speaks their own message. Existing AAC/Live Speech tools already compose/speak; student-controlled hold-my-turn workflow may help, but classroom adoption and AAC compatibility need direct user testing. AI should not invent opinions for the student.

## Decision recommendation
Pick instruction access if we cannot validate a niche need today. Pick chart access if we can test with an actual screen-reader user or accessibility specialist and a tightly scoped fixture. Small population does not imply small individual impact. Do not stretch prevalence figures into projected users helped. Avoid an AI therapist, diagnosis inference, emotion surveillance, automatic accommodation eligibility, or claiming disability support is replaced.
